Kunming is Yunnan’s usual gateway. It is where most international flights land, where you reset after a long journey, and where the high-speed rail west toward Dali begins.

It is not the reason most first-time visitors come to Yunnan. Treat it as the practical start of the route, not a city you have to “complete” before you are allowed to continue.

I live here. That makes Kunming easy to over-explain. For a first week, the useful advice is narrower: land, sleep, decide whether the Stone Forest deserves a day, then move west.

## What Kunming is good for

Kunming works well when you need:

- An international or connecting arrival into Yunnan
- A gentle first night after a long-haul flight
- High-speed rail to Dali the next morning
- A Stone Forest day if your schedule has room

It is less useful if you try to turn it into the scenic highlight of a six- or eight-day trip. The landscapes that change the feel of Yunnan—Erhai, the Tea Horse Road towns, and the mountains around Lijiang—sit west of here.

## How many days in Kunming?

**Arrive and continue the same day:** possible only if your flight lands early and you already have train tickets. I rarely recommend it after an international arrival.

**One night:** the standard first-visit plan. Rest, eat, sort payments and data if anything is still unfinished, then take the high-speed train to Dali.

**Two nights:** worth it if you want the Stone Forest, or if you landed late and need a real recovery day. Two nights is not required for a first Yunnan loop.

**Three nights or more:** only if Kunming itself is the point of the trip, or you are using it as a base before a longer Yunnan circuit. Most first visitors get more from those extra nights in Dali or Shaxi.

For how this sits in a wider first route, use the [Yunnan travel guide](/china-destinations/yunnan). The usual direction is Kunming → Dali → Shaxi → Lijiang.

## The Stone Forest

The Stone Forest (Shilin) is Kunming’s most famous nearby landscape: tall grey limestone pinnacles, paths between the rocks, and—on a still day—reflections in the water at the edge of the site.

It is not in the city. Plan **about 90 minutes each way** by road, plus time on site. That makes it a full day, not a morning errand before a Dali train.

Go if:

- You have a spare day in Kunming
- Dramatic karst landscapes interest you more than another city walk
- You would rather see this than spend the same day shopping or circling Green Lake twice

Skip or shorten it if:

- You only have one night and a morning train
- Your week is already tight around Dali, Shaxi and Lijiang
- You prefer lakes and villages to a major scenic park

![Stone Forest limestone pinnacles near Kunming](/assets/blog/kunming-travel-guide/cover-stone-forest.webp)

Tickets, opening hours and the exact park circuit change. Check the current arrangement the day before, and allow extra time around Chinese public holidays.

## How Kunming connects west

The high-speed train from Kunming to Dali is the reason this city works as a gateway. The journey is roughly **two hours**. You do not need a domestic flight for that stretch.

A simple first-trip shape:

1. Land at Kunming Changshui International Airport
2. Overnight in the city, close to a metro line if you want an easy station transfer
3. Optional Stone Forest day
4. High-speed rail to Dali
5. Continue by road to Shaxi, then Lijiang

If you would rather have those transfers arranged, the [6-day private Yunnan journey](/journeys/kunming-dali-shaxi-lijiang-6-days) follows this same direction. It is a starting route, not a fixed package.

## What to do with one night

Keep the first evening light. After a long flight, Kunming is better as a reset than as a sightseeing test.

Reasonable uses of a single night:

- A walk around **Green Lake** if you arrive with daylight
- A proper meal, then sleep
- Checking that Alipay, data and maps actually work before you leave the larger city

Do not try to add the Stone Forest, a museum circuit and an old-street checklist to that same night. You will start the Dali train tired.

## Practical notes

Kunming is milder than Lijiang and the northwest. That “spring city” reputation is about the urban basin, not the whole province. You can land here in a light jacket and still need a proper coat two days later in Lijiang.

Airport to the city takes time. Changshui is not a close-in airport. Build a buffer before any same-day train.

Foreign-passport hotels still need to be able to register you. Confirm that before you pay, especially if you choose a small guesthouse.

Payments, eSIM and maps are easier to finish in Kunming than in a smaller town. If anything in the [China pre-departure checklist](/survival-kit) is still open, do it here.

## Should Kunming be in your first Yunnan week?

**Include it as the arrival city.** That is the default for most passports and flight networks.

**Give it one night, two if the Stone Forest matters.** Then spend the real time west of here.

**Do not pad the city because it is the capital.** Yunnan rewards a coherent direction more than extra nights in the first place you land.
