Kunming is Yunnan’s usual gateway. It is where most international flights land, where you reset after a long journey, and where the high-speed rail west toward Dali begins.

It is not the reason most first-time visitors come to Yunnan. Treat it as the practical start of the route, not a city you have to “complete” before you are allowed to continue.

I live here. That makes Kunming easy to over-explain. For a first week, the useful advice is narrower: land, sleep in the right part of town, decide whether the Stone Forest deserves a day, then move west.

## What Kunming is good for

Kunming works well when you need:

- An international or connecting arrival into Yunnan
- A gentle first night after a long-haul flight
- High-speed rail to Dali the next morning
- A Stone Forest day if your schedule has room

It is less useful if you try to turn it into the scenic highlight of a six-day trip. The landscapes that change the feel of Yunnan—Erhai, the Tea Horse Road towns, and the mountains around Lijiang—sit west of here.

The eight-day [Essence of Yunnan](/journeys/kunming-dali-lijiang-shangri-la-private-tour) is the exception that still proves the rule: it uses Kunming at both ends because the journey returns to the same airport, not because the capital should eat extra nights on a shorter westbound route.

## How many days in Kunming?

**Arrive and continue the same day:** possible only if your flight lands early and you already have train tickets. I rarely recommend it after an international arrival.

**One night:** the standard first-visit plan. Rest, eat, sort payments and data if anything is still unfinished, then take the high-speed train to Dali.

**Two nights:** worth it if you want the Stone Forest, or if you landed late and need a real recovery day. Two nights is not required for a first Yunnan loop.

**Three nights or more:** only if Kunming itself is the point of the trip, or you are using it as a base before a longer Yunnan circuit. Most first visitors get more from those extra nights in Dali or Shaxi.

For how this sits in a wider first route, use the [Yunnan travel guide](/china-destinations/yunnan). The usual direction is Kunming → Dali → Shaxi → Lijiang.

## Where to stay in Kunming

There is no single “best area.” Match the hotel to your flight time and to the station printed on your Dali ticket.

| Area | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Green Lake / city center** | A first night with daylight, a walk, restaurants, and a sense of everyday Kunming | You still need a morning transfer to the train; confirm which station you actually leave from |
| **Near the station on your ticket** | An early Dali train, or a late arrival with no energy to go into town | The area around a station is practical, not scenic. Kunming Station and Kunming South are different places |
| **Airport hotel at Changshui** | A very late landing or a very early next-day departure | Changshui is not a close-in airport. You skip the city entirely |

If you arrive with daylight and leave the next morning, **Green Lake** is the stay I would choose. It is the easiest way to feel you have landed in Yunnan rather than in a transport corridor. A walk around the lake — tai chi groups, card games, musicians on an ordinary morning — tells you more about Kunming than most single sights would.

If your Dali train is early, do not book a hotel “near the train” in the abstract. High-speed services to Dali leave from **Kunming Station** (昆明站) or **Kunming South** (昆明南站). Check the ticket, then stay on the side of the city that matches it. The two stations are not a short walk from each other.

Use an **airport hotel** only when the clock is against you. Changshui sits well outside the city; the transfer into town takes time, and a same-day train after an international arrival is the usual way people start the trip tired.

Foreign-passport hotels still need to be able to register you. Confirm that before you pay, especially if you choose a small guesthouse. The [hotels for foreigners](/hotels-in-china-for-foreigners) guide covers what that check actually means.

## The Stone Forest

The Stone Forest (Shilin) is Kunming’s most famous nearby landscape: tall grey limestone pinnacles, paths between the rocks, and—on a still day—reflections in the water at the edge of the site.

It is not in the city. Plan **about 90 minutes each way** by road, plus time on site. That makes it a full day, not a morning errand before a Dali train, and not a stop you squeeze in on the way to the airport.

Go if:

- You have a spare day in Kunming
- Dramatic karst landscapes interest you more than another city walk
- You would rather see this than spend the same day shopping or circling Green Lake twice

Skip or shorten it if:

- You only have one night and a morning train
- Your week is already tight around Dali, Shaxi and Lijiang
- You prefer lakes and villages to a major scenic park

![Stone Forest limestone pinnacles near Kunming](/assets/blog/kunming-travel-guide/cover-stone-forest.webp)

It is a large paid scenic area, usually visited as the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest together. Weekends and Chinese public holidays get busy. Tickets, opening hours and the exact park circuit change, so check the current arrangement the day before rather than treating an old blog price as current.

The Stone Forest is optional on a first Yunnan week. It is not the reason to come to Yunnan, and it is not required before you are allowed to continue west.

## How this fits the 6-day and 8-day journeys

The two published private journeys use Kunming differently. That is deliberate.

**[First Encounter Yunnan](/journeys/kunming-dali-shaxi-lijiang-6-days) (6 days):** Kunming is a gateway, not a hotel base. Day 1 is the high-speed train to Dali; the first night is beside Erhai, not in the capital. Add a Kunming hotel night *before* Day 1 if your flight lands in the afternoon or evening. Do not add extra Kunming sightseeing nights in the middle of this route — those days belong in Dali or Shaxi.

**[The Essence of Yunnan](/journeys/kunming-dali-lijiang-shangri-la-private-tour) (8 days):** the journey starts and finishes at Kunming airport, then returns from Shangri-La. That is why it includes more Kunming time: an arrival night, a return night, and a city day at the Western Hills, Dianchi Lake (including Haigeng Dam, where winter gulls often gather) and Guanshang Old Street. Those stops are a coherent way to use the last full day before a Kunming departure. They are not a template for padding a shorter westbound trip.

If you are building your own dates, use the 6-day pattern unless you already know you will fly out of Kunming after Shangri-La. A tailor-made route can add or skip Kunming nights once the flights are fixed.

## How Kunming connects west

The high-speed train from Kunming to Dali is the reason this city works as a gateway. The journey is roughly **two hours**. You do not need a domestic flight for that stretch.

A simple first-trip shape:

1. Land at Kunming Changshui International Airport
2. Overnight in the city — Green Lake if you have daylight, or near the station on your ticket if the train is early
3. Optional Stone Forest day
4. High-speed rail to Dali
5. Continue by road to Shaxi, then Lijiang

## What to do with one night

Keep the first evening light. After a long flight, Kunming is better as a reset than as a sightseeing test.

Reasonable uses of a single night:

- A walk around **Green Lake** if you arrive with daylight
- A proper meal, then sleep
- Checking that Alipay, data and maps actually work before you leave the larger city

Do not try to add the Stone Forest, a museum circuit and an old-street checklist to that same night. You will start the Dali train tired.

## Practical notes

Kunming is milder than Lijiang and the northwest. That “spring city” reputation is about the urban basin, not the whole province. You can land here in a light jacket and still need a proper coat two days later in Lijiang.

Airport to the city takes time. Changshui is not a close-in airport. Build a buffer before any same-day train.

Payments, eSIM and maps are easier to finish in Kunming than in a smaller town. If anything in the [China pre-departure checklist](/survival-kit) is still open, do it here.

## Should Kunming be in your first Yunnan week?

**Include it as the arrival city.** That is the default for most passports and flight networks.

**Give it one night, two if the Stone Forest matters.** On the 8-day Essence route, the extra Kunming time is the return to the airport, not a second city holiday.

**Do not pad the city because it is the capital.** Yunnan rewards a coherent direction more than extra nights in the first place you land.
