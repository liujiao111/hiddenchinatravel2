Dali is worth visiting if you want to slow down—not if you want to collect every Yunnan landmark in 24 hours.

The city sits between Cangshan and Erhai Lake. Its best days are not usually the loudest ones: a walk through the old town before the holiday crowds arrive, a stretch of the lakeside ecological corridor, a village meal away from the souvenir streets, or coffee under a very blue Yunnan sky.

For a first independent visit, I recommend **three nights**. That gives you one old-town day, one Erhai day, and a third day to leave open for Cangshan, Xizhou, or simply a slower morning. Trying to fit Dali, Erhai, Cangshan, Xizhou, Shuanglang, and Lijiang into one or two nights turns the place into a transport problem.

If you are building a first Yunnan week, Dali usually fits between Kunming and Lijiang. See the practical [Kunming–Dali–Lijiang route](/china-destinations/yunnan) before treating it as a separate day trip.

## What Dali is known for

There are four reasons Dali works especially well for independent visitors:

- **Erhai Lake** is the reason to slow down. You do not need to “complete” the whole lake circuit to have a good day.
- **Dali Ancient Town** is easy to walk, eat in, and use as a first base.
- **Bai culture and village life** are more interesting when you step beyond the main souvenir streets.
- **Cangshan** gives the lake basin its scale. Treat it as a weather-dependent mountain day, not something you have to force into every plan.

![Farmhouses and fields below Cangshan in the Dali basin, Yunnan](/assets/blog/dali-travel-guide/cangshan-village-fields.webp)

The common mistake is to treat all four as checklist items. A better Dali day has one main direction: old town, one stretch of Erhai, a village, or the mountain. Leave room for the part that makes Dali different—the fact that you do not need to hurry.

## Is Dali worth visiting?

### Choose Dali if you want

- A slower stop between Kunming and Lijiang
- Lake views, village roads, cafés, and time outside a tour bus schedule
- A base where you can walk for food and use a taxi or private driver for longer days
- A first independent Yunnan trip with fewer hotel changes

### Shorten Dali or reconsider it if you want

- To see every famous Yunnan sight in a few days
- A high-altitude mountain highlight as the main point of the trip
- Packed nightlife or a new landmark every hour
- To make a single rushed stop before continuing to Lijiang

Dali can be busy and commercial, especially around public holidays and the main streets of the old town. That does not make it skippable. It means you should use the old town for what it does well—walking, everyday convenience, and an easy first base—then go beyond it for quieter scenery and food.

## How many days do you need in Dali?

**One night:** possible only as a rail stop. You will get an evening old-town walk, not a real sense of the lake or villages.

**Two nights:** enough for Dali Ancient Town and one focused Erhai day. This is the minimum I would suggest if your Yunnan route is tight.

**Three nights:** the best first-visit pace. Spend one day in and around the old town, one near Erhai, and keep the third flexible for Cangshan, Xizhou, or rest.

**Four nights or more:** worthwhile if you like cycling, photography, village stays, or a slower route with no pressure to cover the entire lake.

## Where to stay in Dali

There is no single “best area.” Choose a base based on how you want your days to feel.

| Area | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dali Ancient Town** | First visits, walkable food, cafés, easy day-to-day convenience | Busy, commercial, and noisy on some streets and holidays |
| **Caicun / west shore of Erhai** | A quieter stay near the ecological corridor and lake scenery | Taxis can be less convenient; plan longer journeys ahead |
| **Xiaguan** | City services and practical transport connections | Farther from the old-town-and-lake atmosphere most visitors came for |
| **Xizhou or another village base** | A slower, more local-feeling stay | Less convenient if you want to move around frequently |
| **Shuanglang / east shore** | Open lake views and a more resort-like stay | Better for a deliberate lake stay than a short, transit-heavy visit |

![Looking down a pedestrian street in Dali Ancient Town toward Cangshan and Erhai](/assets/blog/dali-travel-guide/dali-ancient-town-rooftops.webp)

If you are torn, stay in **Dali Ancient Town**. It is easy for a first visit: meals and basic services are close, and you can arrange a car day when you want to reach Erhai villages or Cangshan. Avoid choosing a room directly on the loudest bar streets if sleep matters.

For the full area-by-area breakdown, the Ancient Town vs Erhai decision, and what to confirm before booking, see [where to stay in Dali](/where-to-stay-in-dali).

## Erhai: do less, see more

Erhai is not a ride you need to finish in a day. The west-side ecological corridor near Caicun is especially good for walking or cycling, but it is not a road for a private car to drive through. Inside the corridor, use the operator's designated bicycle or assisted bike, or the sightseeing electric shuttle—do not assume a privately rented or ordinary shared e-bike may enter. Choose a manageable section, bring water and sun protection, and leave enough battery and daylight for the return.

![Small fishing boat on Erhai Lake with Cangshan across the water](/assets/blog/dali-travel-guide/erhai-lake-cangshan.webp)

You have a few reasonable ways to see the lake:

- **Walk or cycle a short west-shore section** if your goal is the corridor and scenery rather than distance.
- **Use a taxi or DiDi for point-to-point journeys** outside restricted lakeside sections. Keep destination names saved in Chinese in your map app.
- **Arrange a private driver for a flexible day** if you want villages and east-shore viewpoints without trying to solve every connection. In my experience, a day is often quoted around **RMB 300–500**, but treat that as a starting reference only: confirm the route, waiting time, vehicle, and current price directly with the driver or your guesthouse.

A good guesthouse host can often suggest a local driver. Before confirming, check exactly which villages are included, how long the driver will wait, and whether parking or corridor restrictions change the plan.

Do not decide you have “done Erhai” just because you hired a car for a full loop. Pick a side, stop when the place feels good, and allow time to walk.

## Dali Ancient Town: useful, busy, still worth it

Dali Ancient Town is commercial in places, and holiday crowds can be heavy. It is still the most practical base for many independent visitors: you can walk to food, find cafés, buy simple supplies, and start a day without needing a car.

![Wuhua Tower in Dali Ancient Town, with a clear blue sky](/assets/blog/dali-travel-guide/dali-ancient-town-wuhua-tower.webp)

Go early or walk the quieter side streets rather than treating the central shopping streets as the whole town. The souvenir stalls are not where I would buy silver, gifts, or fruit. If you want something more local, look in small lanes and village shops away from the main scenic-area flow.

The same applies to food. The old town has convenient choices, but much of it is designed for visitors. For a more local meal, look along the Erhai route, in Xiaguan, or around Xizhou rather than assuming the busiest old-town restaurant is the best representation of Bai food.

The Three Pagodas are an optional paid stop north of the old town—not a required part of a good Dali day. Check the current entry rules and opening time before going, particularly during public holidays. ## Dali or Lijiang?

The choice is less about which city is “better” and more about what you want from Yunnan.

Choose **Dali** for Erhai, low-pressure days, cafés, cycling, village roads, and a relaxed lake-and-mountain setting.

Choose **Lijiang** if the bigger landscape is your priority: Jade Dragon Snow Mountain and Blue Moon Valley are more dramatic mountain-day experiences, and the wider Lijiang area gives you quieter old towns and villages to explore beyond the central lanes.

With about a week, the better answer is often both—not as a race, but as the [Kunming–Dali–Lijiang route](/china-destinations/yunnan) does it: arrive in Kunming, take the train west, give Dali several nights, then continue to Lijiang. Do not bolt on every Yunnan region just because it appears on a map.

## How to get to Dali and move around

Most independent visitors reach Dali by high-speed rail from Kunming or via a shorter connection from Lijiang. Flights and local buses also exist, but schedules, stops, and seasonal services change, so check the actual service for your arrival date rather than relying on an old blog timetable.

From Dali Railway Station or Dali Fengyi Airport, ask the service desk whether a current shuttle serves your destination; some routes require a change rather than going directly to the old town, Caicun, or another accommodation area. If the schedule does not line up, DiDi or a normal taxi is the straightforward fallback—especially with luggage or a late arrival.

Before travel day:

1. Confirm the **exact station or airport**, not just “Dali.”
2. Save your hotel name and address in **Chinese characters**.
3. Set up data and a payment backup before you land.
4. Build some buffer into your schedule; a missed connection should not erase your only Erhai day.

Use [Transport in China](/transport-in-china) for the broader rail system, [how to book high-speed rail tickets](/book-china-high-speed-rail-foreigners) before you purchase, and [how to use DiDi as a foreign visitor](/how-to-use-didi-china-foreigners) for the local ride-hailing setup.

## What to prepare before you arrive

Independent Dali travel is practical, but its friction is mostly digital. Do these before your first train or flight:

- **Payments:** Set up [Alipay](/alipay-for-foreigners-china) and carry a small cash backup. QR payment is normal, but it is not a reason to arrive with no fallback.
- **Data:** Install an [eSIM for China](/best-esim-for-china-travel) before you fly. You will need it for maps, ride-hailing, bookings, and translation.
- **Maps:** Save Chinese place names and use the right [maps and navigation setup](/maps-navigation-in-china). A Google Maps screenshot is not enough for a village lane or the correct station exit.
- **Hotels:** Confirm that the property can check in foreign passport holders; [this hotel guide](/hotels-in-china) explains the questions to ask.
- **Sun protection:** Dali rewards slow outdoor days, but the Yunnan sun is strong. Carry sunscreen, water, and a layer for changing lake or mountain weather.
