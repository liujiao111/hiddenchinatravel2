**Quick answer:** If you hold a Singapore ordinary passport, you can visit mainland China visa-free for up to **30 days**. For a first Yunnan trip, compare flights from Singapore to Kunming, then plan an unhurried route through Dali, Shaxi and Lijiang. Allow around eight or nine calendar days if international travel is included; a six-day Yunnan journey needs arrival and departure time on top.

For the broader province-wide view, start with the [Yunnan travel guide](/china-destinations/yunnan). This page focuses on the decisions that are different when you start in Singapore.

## Do Singapore citizens need a visa for China?

**No visa is required for a short holiday if you hold a Singapore ordinary passport.** The reciprocal Singapore–China visa exemption took effect on 9 February 2024 and allows stays of up to 30 days for purposes including tourism, business and visiting family. It covers Yunnan and entry through Kunming. Longer stays and activities such as employment or journalism have different requirements.

| Entry question | Answer for Singapore ordinary passport holders |
| --- | --- |
| Visa for a short Yunnan holiday | No |
| Maximum stay under this arrangement | 30 days per entry |
| Kunming entry and Yunnan travel | Covered |
| Longer stay or work | Check the appropriate visa before travel |

This is a **bilateral agreement**, separate from China's transit visa-free rules. You do not need to arrange a third-country transit itinerary for a straightforward Singapore–Yunnan holiday. Entry policies can change, so check the [Singapore Ministry of Foreign Affairs travel page](https://www.mfa.gov.sg/Countries-Regions/C/China/Travel-Page) and your airline's document requirements before you leave.

**Living in Singapore but travelling on another passport?** The rule depends on your nationality and passport, not where you depart from. Start with our [China visa guide](/do-i-need-a-visa-for-china), then confirm the rule for your passport with the relevant Chinese embassy.

## How to get from Singapore to Yunnan

**Kunming Changshui International Airport (KMG)** is the first airport to check. Nonstop services from Singapore (SIN) to Kunming may be available on your dates, with a flying time of roughly four hours. Flight schedules, airlines and fares change, so compare both directions before fixing your hotel nights.

For Dali (DLU) and Lijiang (LJG), expect to compare connecting flights. A short connection can look attractive in search results, but immigration, baggage and a change of terminal may make the overall day much longer.

| Arrival airport | How it fits a first trip | Check before booking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kunming (KMG)** | Natural start for Kunming → Dali → Shaxi → Lijiang | Nonstop availability and arrival time |
| **Dali (DLU)** | Useful if you plan to skip Kunming | Connection length and onward transport |
| **Lijiang (LJG)** | Better for a northwest-only trip or your flight home | Connection and return fare |

**My pick:** arrive in Kunming. For the flight home, price two options: Lijiang → Singapore with a connection, or travel back to Kunming and fly from there. The first saves backtracking; the second may be simpler or cheaper on your dates. Compare the *whole* journey rather than just the flight price.

## How many days should you spend in Yunnan?

Count your arrival and departure days. A route advertised as **six days in Yunnan** does not automatically include your flight from Singapore, an overnight before it starts, or the journey home.

| Total calendar days from Singapore | A workable plan |
| --- | --- |
| **5–6** | Choose two bases; leave Shaxi or Lijiang for another trip |
| **7** | Focus on Kunming, Dali and Lijiang, or keep Shaxi to one night with arranged transfers |
| **8–9** | Kunming, Dali, an overnight in Shaxi and Lijiang at a more comfortable pace |
| **10+** | Slow down further or consider another stop after checking transfer times |

### Should you add Shangri-La?

For a week-long trip, I would skip it. Shangri-La sits above 3,000 metres and adds travel time and an altitude adjustment. With ten or more days, it becomes more realistic, especially if you are happy to cut time elsewhere or book a different return route.

## Is Yunnan worth the trip from Singapore?

Yunnan makes sense if you want mountain air, lakeside villages, old towns and slower days instead of another city break. Kunming is the gateway; Dali is where you can spend a morning beside Erhai; Shaxi is worth staying in after the day visitors leave; and Lijiang gives you access to the northwest's mountain scenery.

![Cangshan mountains and Erhai Lake near Dali in Yunnan](/assets/blog/dali-travel-guide/erhai-lake-cangshan.webp)

The trade-off is time in transit. Distances within Yunnan are larger than they look on a map. A first trip feels better when you stay overnight in the places you care about, rather than changing towns just to tick them off.

## An 8-day first-time route from Singapore

**Singapore → Kunming → Dali → Shaxi → Lijiang → Singapore** is a sensible shape for an eight-day trip. The day-by-day outline is a planning example, not a flight or train timetable.

| Day | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | Fly to Kunming; stay overnight and keep the evening free |
| **2** | Travel from Kunming to Dali; settle in near the Old Town or Erhai |
| **3** | Give Dali a full day for the lake, a village or the Old Town |
| **4** | Travel by road to Shaxi; stay overnight |
| **5** | Enjoy a slower Shaxi morning, then continue toward Lijiang |
| **6–7** | Explore Lijiang and its surroundings without changing hotels again |
| **8** | Fly home via a suitable connection, or return to Kunming earlier if your ticket requires it |

![A bridge over the river near Shaxi, between Dali and Lijiang](/assets/journeys/kunming-dali-shaxi-lijiang-6-days/shaxi-bridge-river-view.webp)

If your departure is from Kunming, protect enough time for the return transfer: you may need to move that transfer to Day 7 and shorten Lijiang. If you have only seven calendar days, dropping a stop is usually better than squeezing every transfer into a single week.
