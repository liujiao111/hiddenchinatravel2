Planning a China trip independently is less “pick 12 famous names” and more “choose a pace you can actually walk.” High-speed rail makes long distances look easy on a map. Station time, hotel check-in, and first-day app setup do not.

This guide is the planning layer. For whether independent travel is even realistic, start with [independent travel in China](/independent-travel-china). For payments, data, and real-name bookings, keep the [essentials hub](/china-travel-essentials) open — those systems should be in motion **before** you polish café-level days.

If you already know your dates and want a local to turn a skeleton into a ready-to-follow PDF, use the [itinerary planner](/china-itinerary-planner#plan-trip) or read what is included on the [custom itinerary page](/services/custom-itinerary).

## Plan in this order (not the reverse)

A workable independent itinerary has four layers. Most first-timers start on layer four.

1. **Entry.** Visa-free, transit, or a tourist visa — passport-specific. Use [Do I Need a Visa for China?](/do-i-need-a-visa-for-china) and the [visa checker](/china-visa-checker).
2. **Systems.** [Payments](/payments-in-china) and [internet](/internet-in-china) decide whether day one is calm. Maps, DiDi, and QR menus sit on that stack.
3. **Skeleton.** Overnight cities, rail hops, and one buffer night. This is the itinerary.
4. **Days.** Neighborhoods, workshops, one landmark that actually fits. Optional once 1–3 are honest.

If you reverse the order, you get a beautiful spreadsheet that dies at the first failed Alipay QR.

## How many cities for the days you have

Treat **overnight bases**, not “places you passed on a train,” as cities.

| Trip length | Overnight bases that usually work | What to cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 5 days | 1, maybe 2 | A third city almost never fits |
| 6–10 days | 2–3 (a rural corridor between them is a stretch, not another “greatest hits” city) | Five-city sprint loops |
| 10+ days | 3–4, or 2 plus a slower rural stretch | A new city every night |

A useful test: if two cities are more than about four hours of rail apart, they each need a real night — not a “quick look.” [High-speed rail](/book-china-high-speed-rail-foreigners) is the backbone, not a teleport.

Beijing → Xi’an → Chengdu in ten days is possible and still rushed. Beijing → Xi’an → Chengdu → Zhangjiajie → Shanghai in the same window is a tour product pretending to be independent travel.

## Build the skeleton: rail, not a sightseeing list

Write the trip as **nights**, then hang trains on those nights.

- Pick a **start city** that matches your international flight (or accept one domestic hop).
- Pick an **end city** the same way. Do not “figure out the exit later.”
- Connect them with **one rail spine**. Side trips should return to the spine or replace a night on it — not stack new hubs.
- Put **one buffer night** near the end. Sold-out trains and weather are normal.

Example shape (not a promise, not a client trip): fly into Chongqing, overnight there, then a slower corridor, then one landscape stop, then back to the same airport city. That is how a [sample southwest loop](/services/custom-itinerary) is sketched — labeled as a sample on purpose.

A first independent week often looks simpler than that: fly into Kunming, then fast trains to Dali and Lijiang. [Independent Yunnan travel](/china-destinations/yunnan) is the worked example — days, trains, and when not to bolt on Xishuangbanna. If Dali is the base and you want days outside the Old Town, [Dali hidden gems](/dali-hidden-gems-off-the-beaten-path) covers Shaxi, Weishan, Xizhou, and the rest.

For trains and stations, use the [transport hub](/transport-in-china). For night-one lodging that accepts a foreign passport, use [hotels in China](/hotels-in-china). Iconic sights that need passport reservations belong in [attraction tickets](/attraction-tickets-in-china) — not in a walk-up fantasy.

## What to leave unplanned

Independent travel in China feels better when some days are **direction, not a timetable**.

Leave loose:

- First afternoon after a long-haul flight
- One market / neighborhood day in each base city
- Weather-sensitive landscape days (have an indoor backup, not a second city)

Lock early:

- Overnight hotels that accept foreigners
- Peak-season attraction reservations
- Long-distance rail on Fridays and holiday weeks

You do not need a 6 a.m. museum slot on day one to prove the trip is “efficient.”

## DIY sketch vs a local PDF

**DIY** is enough when your cities are already chosen, your dates are fixed, and you mainly need booking order. Follow the essentials hub, then this skeleton.

**A custom PDF** is worth it when:

- You mix cities with countryside or minority regions and do not want a staged “park”
- You have 8–14 days and keep adding cities because the map looks small
- Photography, food, or family pace matters more than covering famous names
- You want someone local to sanity-check trains and overnight placement

## A one-page checklist before you buy tickets

- Entry path checked for **your** passport
- eSIM / SIM and a VPN backup decided
- Alipay or WeChat Pay path started (plus a cash backup)
- Overnight cities listed — count nights, not pins
- Rail hops drafted with a buffer
- Night-one hotel that takes a foreign passport
- Only the tickets that actually require advance reservation

When that list is honest, the itinerary is already most of the way there. The rest is taste: old streets vs museums, one landscape vs another, how early you want to get up.
