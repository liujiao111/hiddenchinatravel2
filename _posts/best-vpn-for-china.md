## VPN or eSIM: What Do You Actually Need?

This is one of the most important questions — because **a VPN and an international eSIM solve different problems.**

A VPN changes how your internet traffic is routed. An international travel eSIM, on the other hand, may provide mobile data through an international roaming network. That distinction matters in China.

### You may want a VPN if:

* You're connecting to hotel or café Wi-Fi.
* You need access to websites or services restricted on mainland Chinese networks.
* You want a VPN available regardless of which local Wi-Fi network you're using.
* You already have a Chinese SIM or local data plan.

### You may prefer an international eSIM if:

* You mainly need mobile internet while traveling.
* You don't want to configure a VPN.
* You want your mobile data connection to avoid relying entirely on local Wi-Fi.
* You want a simpler setup for a short trip.

For many travelers, **having both is the most convenient setup**.

The eSIM gives you mobile connectivity, while the VPN gives you another way to access services that may not work normally on mainland networks.

> **Simple China internet setup**
>
> **VPN:** helps with access to restricted services and gives you another option when using Wi-Fi.
>
> **International eSIM:** gives you mobile data and reduces your dependence on hotel Wi-Fi.
>
> **Both:** gives you more than one way to get online.
>
> If you're traveling to China for the first time, this is the setup I would consider rather than relying on a single connection.

## How I Evaluate VPNs for China

I don't think "best VPN" should simply mean the VPN with the fastest speed test or the cheapest monthly price.

For China travel, I care more about whether the VPN is practical when you actually need it.

My main criteria are:

### 1. Connection reliability

Can it actually establish a connection from a restrictive network?

### 2. Connection time

Does it connect quickly, or do you regularly find yourself staring at "Connecting..."?

### 3. Stability

Once connected, does the connection remain usable?

### 4. Speed

Can you browse, use Google services, send messages, upload photos, and make calls without constantly fighting the connection?

### 5. Ease of setup

Can an ordinary traveler install and use it without understanding VPN protocols?

### 6. Backup options

If the default connection doesn't work, does the app give you other options to try?

This is why I don't think there's a permanent "number one VPN for China."

The network environment changes, VPN providers change their technology, and your hotel Wi-Fi may behave completely differently from someone else's.

## What About Hotel Wi-Fi?

Hotel Wi-Fi is one of the most common places where travelers discover that their internet setup isn't as simple as expected.

A typical situation looks like this:

1. You arrive at your hotel.
2. You connect to Wi-Fi.
3. The hotel requires a login page.
4. You connect your VPN.
5. The VPN doesn't work.
6. You assume the VPN is broken.

Before troubleshooting the VPN, make sure the hotel Wi-Fi itself is fully connected.

Open a normal website first.

If the hotel has a captive portal, complete the hotel login.

Then turn on the VPN.

If it still doesn't work, try:

* Disconnecting and reconnecting to Wi-Fi
* Switching VPN servers
* Restarting the VPN app
* Trying another protocol
* Using mobile data temporarily
* Connecting through an eSIM instead of hotel Wi-Fi

For ExpressVPN, the current official troubleshooting flow recommends trying another server location and changing protocols if necessary.

## What If My VPN Doesn't Connect?

Don't immediately assume you need a new VPN.

Try these steps in order.

### 1. Check the internet connection

Make sure the underlying Wi-Fi or mobile data actually works.

### 2. Try another server

A temporary server-side issue can sometimes look like a VPN failure.

### 3. Restart the app

Simple, but surprisingly useful.

### 4. Change the protocol

For ExpressVPN, Automatic is the recommended starting point. If that doesn't work, you can try alternative protocols available on your device.

### 5. Switch networks

Try:

**Hotel Wi-Fi → mobile data**

or:

**Mobile data → hotel Wi-Fi**

If the VPN works on one but not the other, the network itself may be the problem.

### 6. Use your backup

This is exactly why I recommend not putting all your connectivity eggs in one basket.

> **If ExpressVPN doesn't connect**
>
> Don't immediately assume the VPN is useless.
>
> First try another server, restart the app, change the protocol, or switch networks.
>
> If internet access is important, use your backup connection as well.
