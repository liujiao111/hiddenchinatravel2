## Do Australian Citizens Need a Visa for China?

**Quick answer:** As of September 2026, Australian ordinary passport holders can enter mainland China visa-free for eligible visits of up to 30 days. This is a unilateral policy set by China, not a permanent one. It's currently scheduled to run until 31 December 2026, and China's immigration authorities make the final decision on entry in every case.

### What the visa-free policy covers

The 30-day waiver applies to tourism, business, visiting family and friends, exchanges, and transit. That covers the large majority of Australians planning a Yunnan holiday, including combining Yunnan with time in another Chinese city.

### When Australians may still need a visa

You'll generally still need a visa, rather than relying on the waiver, if:

- your trip runs longer than 30 days
- you're travelling to work or study in China
- your purpose isn't one of the categories the waiver covers
- you're travelling on an official or diplomatic passport
- the policy has expired or changed by the time you travel

### What to prepare at immigration

Immigration officers decide entry case by case, and requirements can vary. It's sensible to have ready:

- a valid Australian passport
- a return or onward ticket
- your accommodation details
- a basic outline of your itinerary
- contact details for your first hotel or local organiser

Not every traveller is asked for all of this, but having it on hand avoids delays. For a personalised check, [check the latest China entry rules for your passport](/china-visa-checker).

## Is the 240-Hour Visa-Free Transit Useful for Australians?

China also runs a separate 240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit policy, and Australia is on the eligible list. In practice, it isn't the option most Australians need right now, because the broader 30-day waiver already covers a straightforward China holiday without the routing restrictions transit rules carry.

The 240-hour policy is built for travel from one country or region *through* China to a different third country or region — for example, Australia → China → Singapore. A trip shaped like Australia → China → Australia generally isn't a qualifying transit, since you're not continuing on to a different destination.

This policy could become more relevant if entry rules change, or if you're planning a multi-country trip that genuinely routes through China. Before relying on it, confirm your entry port, the areas you're permitted to visit, that you hold a confirmed onward ticket, and the current rules. No particular airport, route or the whole of Yunnan is automatically covered, so check rather than assume.

## How to Get to Yunnan from Australia

Do not build the trip around a nonstop Australia–Kunming flight unless one appears for your exact dates. Most Australian travellers reach Kunming with one connection through a major Asian or Chinese hub, and exactly which hub makes sense depends on your departure city and the airlines currently flying that route.

### From Sydney

Sydney has the widest set of one-stop options into Kunming of the four cities covered here, routing through hubs like Guangzhou, Shanghai or Chengdu on mainland Chinese carriers, or via Hong Kong or Singapore. Airlines reshuffle these routes each season, so use this as a starting point for your own search rather than a fixed timetable.

### From Melbourne

Melbourne's choice of China and Asia routes runs close to Sydney's, through much the same hubs — Guangzhou, Shanghai, Chengdu, Hong Kong. Carriers and frequencies move around, though, so it's worth running a fresh search rather than assuming last year's flights are still operating.

### From Brisbane

Brisbane has fewer direct international options than Sydney or Melbourne. In practice, that usually means routing through Guangzhou or Hong Kong first, then a second flight on to Kunming.

### From Perth

Perth sits closer to Asia than the eastern capitals, though that doesn't automatically translate into a shorter total journey to Yunnan. It comes down to which hub lines up with your dates. Compare Singapore, Hong Kong and mainland Chinese hubs against each other rather than assuming geography wins on its own.

| Australian departure city | Practical connecting hubs | Likely total journey pattern | Best for |

| Sydney | Guangzhou, Shanghai, Chengdu, Hong Kong | One-stop route to Kunming | Most itinerary flexibility |

## What Is the Best Airport for Yunnan?

### Kunming Changshui International Airport (KMG)

Kunming is the right default for almost every Australian visiting Yunnan for the first time. It's the province's main air gateway, usually has the widest choice of connections from Australia's one-stop routings, and sits at the start of the classic Kunming → Dali → Shaxi → Lijiang route. From Kunming you can continue by high-speed train or a short domestic flight.

### Lijiang Sanyi International Airport

This suits a trip that's entirely focused on Lijiang, Shangri-La or northwest Yunnan, provided you can find a reasonable connecting itinerary straight into Lijiang. Skip it if Kunming is also part of your plans.

### Dali Fengyi Airport

Not a sensible default entry point for Australian travellers, since international connecting options into Dali are typically more limited than into Kunming.

## How Many Days Should Australians Spend in Yunnan?

| Time available | Recommended plan |
|---|---|
| 6 days | Kunming, Dali, Shaxi and Lijiang; keep transfers efficient |
| 7–8 days | Kunming, Dali, Shaxi and Lijiang at a more relaxed pace |
| 8–10 days | Add Shangri-La |
| 10–12 days | Add Shangri-La plus extra time in Dali or Lijiang, or a slower countryside stay |

For most Australians, 6–8 days covers the classic Kunming, Dali, Shaxi and Lijiang route comfortably. A shorter trip is possible, but the long international journey makes a rushed three- or four-day Yunnan circuit poor value. If you want to add Shangri-La, plan for at least 8 days total so the extra driving and altitude change don't feel rushed.

A few Australia-specific factors worth weighing:

- International travel alone will use up close to two days of your trip
- The time difference between Australia and China is relatively small compared with Europe or North America, so jet lag is usually milder
- You'll still want a rest day after the long-haul flight, even with an easier time difference
- Some Australians combine Yunnan with another Chinese city or an Asian stopover, but this article focuses on Yunnan itself rather than a wider national itinerary

### Kunming

Arrival and recovery. A walk around Green Lake is an easy first activity — TripAdvisor reviewers often mention the tai chi groups, card games, musicians and dancing that fill the park on an ordinary morning, which gives a better feel for everyday Kunming than most single sights would. Add the Stone Forest only on the longer end of this range, if your schedule and energy allow — it's not essential to a first Yunnan trip.

![The Stone Forest near Kunming in Yunnan](/assets/blog/yunnan-travel-from-australia/kunming-stone-forest.webp)

### Dali

This is where the trip settles into a slower rhythm. Spend time in Dali Ancient Town, visit villages along Erhai Lake, browse local markets, and leave at least one flexible day around the lake rather than planning every hour. Renting a bike to ride out to Xizhou, a quieter village on the lake, comes up repeatedly in traveller reviews as one of the best half-days of a Dali stay — though a few reviewers also warn that rental bikes vary in quality, so check the brakes before you set off.

![Erhai Lake and the Cangshan mountains near Dali](/assets/blog/yunnan-travel-from-australia/dali-erhai-lake.webp)

### Shaxi

A smaller Tea Horse Road town. Reviewers commonly describe it as "lightyears away from the touristy scene" of Dali or Lijiang, and a quiet respite from the more commercialised ancient towns nearby — which matches what most first-time visitors notice as soon as they arrive. It suits travellers who want something away from the main tourist circuit. The road transfer from Dali needs planning in advance.

![Yujin Bridge over the river in Shaxi](/assets/journeys/kunming-dali-shaxi-lijiang-6-days/shaxi-yujin-bridge-rod-waddington.jpg)

### Lijiang

Explore Lijiang Old Town, then add Baisha or Yuhu for a change of pace if you're on the 8-day version. Reviewers consistently note a sharp contrast between the two ends of the day here: the lanes are close to empty at dawn, then fill in fast from around 6pm as everyone returns from sightseeing and the courtyards turn festive with music and dancing. An early walk before the crowds arrive is worth building into your one flexible day. Depart either from Lijiang directly or via a return connection through Kunming, depending on your flight options.

## Recommended 8+ Day Route: Adding Shangri-La

**Kunming → Dali → Shaxi → Lijiang → Shangri-La**

![Pudacuo National Park near Shangri-La, Yunnan](/assets/blog/yunnan-travel-from-australia/shangri-la-pudacuo.webp)

Adding Shangri-La is worth it once you have at least 8 days total, and the time to do it properly:

- Allow at least two extra days beyond the core 6–8 day route for Shangri-La itself
- Shangri-La sits noticeably higher than Kunming and Dali, and altitude affects people differently
- Slow the pace down rather than scheduling demanding activities on arrival
- Older travellers or anyone with health concerns should check with a doctor before planning the altitude gain

Shangri-La isn't the right add-on for every trip — it depends on your time and how you want to travel.

### Which Route Is Right for You?

- **If you want the easiest first trip:** choose Kunming, Dali and Lijiang.
- **If you prefer smaller towns:** add Shaxi.
- **If you want mountain scenery and Tibetan culture:** add Shangri-La, and allow 8 days or more.
- **If travelling with parents:** slow the route down and spend longer in Dali.
- **If you only have 6–7 days:** skip Shangri-La rather than rushing every stop.

## Best Time to Visit Yunnan from Australia

### March to May

Good for spring scenery and mild weather, though Chinese public holidays around this period can bring larger domestic crowds to popular sites.

### September to November

Generally a strong window for the classic Yunnan route. Days are pleasant, but expect a noticeable drop in temperature after dark, especially in Dali and Lijiang.

### Australian Christmas and New Year holidays

Many parts of Yunnan can be comfortable during the day at this time of year, but mornings and evenings are cold — Lijiang and Shangri-La more so than Kunming. Yunnan isn't warm everywhere year-round, so pack accordingly. Christmas itself isn't a Chinese public holiday, but domestic travel around New Year and the school holiday period can still affect crowds and prices.

### Australian Easter and school holidays

A realistic planning window for many families, though school holiday dates vary by state, so check your own state's calendar rather than assuming a fixed date. It's also worth avoiding or planning carefully around Chinese New Year, the Labour Day holiday and National Day Golden Week, when domestic travel within China increases significantly — check the current year's exact dates before booking.

## Practical China Preparation for Australians

### Payments

Alipay and WeChat Pay handle most day-to-day spending in China. International bank cards can often be linked to either app, though acceptance and verification steps shift from time to time. Carry a backup card and a bit of cash in case yours doesn't link cleanly. See our [payments in China guide](/payments-in-china/), [Alipay for foreigners](/alipay-for-foreigners-china/) and [WeChat Pay for foreigners](/wechat-pay-for-foreigners-china/).

### Internet

Google, WhatsApp, Instagram and a few other overseas services don't load reliably on mainland Chinese networks. International roaming and some travel eSIMs get around this differently, and VPN performance moves around too — none of it's a sure thing on any given day. See our [internet in China guide](/internet-in-china/), [best eSIM for China](/best-esim-for-china-travel/) and [do you need a VPN in China](/do-you-need-vpn-china).

### Maps and transport

Google Maps isn't the most reliable way to navigate once you're in mainland China. High-speed rail covers part of the Kunming–Dali–Lijiang route well, but Shaxi has no station, so that leg means a road transfer either way. A private driver suits anyone with more luggage, travelling as a family, or just wanting a slower pace.
