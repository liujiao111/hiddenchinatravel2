If you are torn, stay in **Dali Ancient Town**. It is the easiest base for a first visit: food, cafés, and basic services are within walking distance, and you can arrange a car or driver for Erhai, Xizhou, or Cangshan without needing one every day.

This is the deep dive on accommodation. For the wider question of how many days Dali needs and how it fits a Yunnan trip, start with the [Dali travel guide](/dali-travel-guide).

## Ancient Town or Erhai: the real decision

Most people are not actually choosing between five areas. They are choosing between two feelings.

**Choose the Ancient Town if:**

- You want to walk to breakfast, dinner, and a café without planning around it
- This is your first trip to Dali and you do not want a transport puzzle
- You are staying two or three nights total

![Pedestrian street in Dali Ancient Town with shops, visitors, and Cangshan in cloud](/assets/blog/where-to-stay-in-dali/dali-ancient-town-street.webp "Dali Ancient Town")

**Choose Erhai's west shore (Caicun and nearby villages) if:**

- A quiet lake view in the morning matters more than convenience
- You are comfortable arranging a taxi, DiDi, or driver for most trips out
- You have four or more nights and can afford a slower day or two

**A common middle path:** spend most nights in the Ancient Town, then move to a lakeside guesthouse for one or two nights near the end of the Dali stop. That gives you the convenience first and the quiet last, without living out of a suitcase every day.

## Area by area

| Area | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dali Ancient Town** | First visits, walkable food, easy transport | Busy and commercial on the main streets, especially on holidays |
| **Caicun / Erhai west shore** | A quieter stay near the lake and the ecological corridor | Taxis and DiDi take longer to arrive; plan trips ahead |
| **Xiaguan** | Transport connections, everyday city services, budget stays | Furthest from the old-town-and-lake atmosphere most visitors came for |
| **Xizhou** | A slower, more local-feeling stay among working villages | Fewer restaurant and shop options right outside your door |
| **Shuanglang / east shore** | Open lake views, a more resort-style stay | An hour or more from Dali by road, more in peak-season traffic — better as a deliberate stay than a quick stop |

![Stone lane in Xizhou Old Town with Bai-style houses and visitors walking](/assets/blog/where-to-stay-in-dali/xizhou-old-town-street.webp "Xizhou Old Town")

![Shuanglang village on a peninsula in Erhai Lake, Dali](/assets/blog/where-to-stay-in-dali/shuanglang-erhai-peninsula.webp)

Nightly prices in Dali move a lot with season and holidays. As a rough starting point: budget guesthouses in the Ancient Town or Xiaguan often fall in the **$20–45** range, and boutique lakeside or design-forward stays can run **$40–90+**. Treat any number here as a planning range, not a quote — confirm the current rate for your dates before booking.

By Erhai, I stayed at [Dali Panshan Tinghai Homestay](/go/dali-panshan-tinghai-homestay) (大理畔山听海民宿), a small guesthouse near Caicun and the ecological corridor. Its Trip.com listing also shows guests from all countries and regions are welcome — again, worth checking for any specific property rather than assuming. The listing also states the guest checking in must be between 18 and 60 years old, so it is not the right fit for every traveler — confirm directly with the property if that could be an issue for your group.

### What to know about Erhai and the west shore

![Lakeside stretch of the Caicun ecological corridor on Erhai, with a performer on the shore](/assets/blog/where-to-stay-in-dali/caicun-ecological-corridor.webp "Caicun ecological corridor")

The lakeside ecological corridor near Caicun is closed to private cars and to ordinary rented scooters and shared e-bikes. Inside the corridor, you use the operator's own bicycles, assisted bikes, or the sightseeing electric shuttle. A car or scooter still gets you to a village entrance near the corridor — it just cannot follow the lakeside path itself. Keep this in mind if a guesthouse's photos show the shoreline right outside the door; check how far the actual walk or ride is from where a car can drop you.

## Foreign-passport check-in: what to confirm before booking

Every hotel in China needs a specific permit to register foreign guests with the local police system. Smaller, family-run guesthouses sometimes do not have it — this is a licensing and paperwork issue, not a judgment about you.

- Filter for foreign-guest acceptance on [Trip.com](/go/trip-hotels) rather than assuming any listing works. Booking.com often cannot confirm this until you are standing at the desk.
- Message the property before you pay if the listing is not explicit about foreign-passport check-in.
- Bring your passport to check-in; registering it with the local police system is standard procedure, not something unusual, and not specific to you.

The full explanation of how this works — and what to do if you are turned away — is in [Hotels in China for foreigners](/hotels-in-china-for-foreigners).

## Payments and cash

Alipay covers most day-to-day spending in Dali: guesthouses, restaurants, cafés, and most market stalls. That said, still carry a small amount of cash. Older market stalls, a private driver settling up in person, or a QR payment that fails at the wrong moment are common enough that a cash backup is worth having, even if you rarely end up using it.

Set up [Alipay](/alipay-for-foreigners-china) before you fly, and see [Payments in China](/payments-in-china) for the fuller backup plan.

## Getting around without a car

You do not need to rent a car to base yourself well in Dali. The Ancient Town works well on foot for daily life; taxis and DiDi cover the longer trips to Erhai villages, Xizhou, or Cangshan.

- Install [Amap](/maps-navigation-in-china) and save your accommodation's name in Chinese characters — Google Maps is not reliable on the mainland.
- Set up [DiDi](/how-to-use-didi-china-foreigners) before you land so ride-hailing works from day one.
- If you want to explore Erhai independently, use bicycles or e-bikes outside the restricted corridor sections, or the corridor's own rental bikes and shuttle inside it.

## A Western-style breakfast option, if you need one

If rice noodles every morning is not for you, there is a Western-style breakfast spot in the Ancient Town listed on Amap as "Serendipity大理美国小馆." Search that name directly rather than relying on an address, and check current hours before you go — small guesthouse-area restaurants change their hours and menus often.

## How long to stay in each area

For **2–3 nights** total, keep the whole stay in or near the Ancient Town. Splitting a short stay across two areas usually costs more time in transfers than it gives back in variety.

For **4–5 nights**, splitting the stay — most nights in the Ancient Town, one or two by the lake — is a workable pattern. See the [Dali travel guide's day-count breakdown](/dali-travel-guide#how-many-days-do-you-need-in-dali) if you have not settled on a number yet.

With **a week or more** in Yunnan, adding a night in Xizhou or on the east shore near Shuanglang is worth considering. Keep the Ancient Town or Caicun as your base for the rest of the stop rather than moving accommodation every night.
