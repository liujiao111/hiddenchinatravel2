Shaxi Ancient Town (沙溪古镇) lies between Dali and Lijiang in western Yunnan. Its old market square, Bai courtyards and stone bridge tell the story of a town that once welcomed traders on the Ancient Tea Horse Road.

It is not an untouched secret. You will find cafés, guesthouses and other visitors here. But compared with racing from attraction to attraction, Shaxi offers something more rewarding: a good reason to slow down.

The historic centre can be walked in a few hours. **I would usually recommend staying at least one night**, particularly if Shaxi is part of your route from [Dali](/dali-travel-guide) to [Lijiang](/lijiang-travel-guide). This guide covers the sights, transport, accommodation and the practical decisions that matter most.

There are other places named Shaxi in China. For maps and bookings, look for **Shaxi in Jianchuan County, Yunnan**, not the similarly named town in Jiangsu.

## Is Shaxi Ancient Town worth visiting?

**Yes, if you enjoy small historic places, local culture and time away from a packed sightseeing schedule.** Shaxi is particularly good for architecture, photography, countryside walks and an overnight stay in a traditional courtyard.

The difference from Dali and Lijiang is not that Shaxi has no tourism. It does, and the historic core can be busy at popular times. The difference is its smaller scale and the possibility of seeing the town in the evening and early morning.

Here is how I think about the three stops:

| Destination | What it does best | Suggested approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dali | Erhai Lake, Bai villages, cafés and a comfortable first base | Spend time by the lake and outside the old-town centre |
| Shaxi | Historic market streets, rural valley and Tea Horse Road atmosphere | Stay overnight instead of just passing through |
| Lijiang | Historic lanes, nearby villages and Jade Dragon Snow Mountain | Keep a separate day for the mountain |

If your trip is only a few days long and your priority is Jade Dragon Snow Mountain or Shangri-La, it may make sense to leave Shaxi for another visit. A good itinerary does not need every possible stop.

## Best things to do in Shaxi Ancient Town

### 1. Walk around Sideng Square and the old theatre

**Sideng Square / Sideng Street (寺登街)** is the historic centre of Shaxi. Traditional shopfronts and courtyards surround the square, where traders once gathered during the Tea Horse Road era.

The old theatre stage is one of the town's most recognisable buildings. Across the square stands Xingjiao Temple. Walk the smaller lanes leading away from the square as well as the central street: the way the buildings fit together is part of what makes this place interesting.

Allow about **one to two hours** to explore the square, nearby lanes and temple area without hurrying.

![Traditional wooden theatre courtyard in Shaxi Ancient Town, Yunnan](/assets/blog/dali-hidden-gems-off-the-beaten-path/shaxi-theater-courtyard.webp)

### 2. Visit Xingjiao Temple

**Xingjiao Temple (兴教寺)** dates to the Ming dynasty and is closely connected to the historic market area. It offers another layer to Shaxi's story beyond the cafés and photo spots.

If you enjoy architecture and local history, make time for the temple. Check its current admission arrangements and opening hours locally; the public square and individual historic buildings do not necessarily have the same access rules.

### 3. Walk to Yujin Bridge

**Yujin Bridge (玉津桥)** crosses the Heihui River just outside the old-town lanes. The landscape opens from timber buildings and stone paving into riverbanks, fields and mountain views.

It is a lovely walk in the softer light of morning or late afternoon. Mist sometimes appears above the river, but do not build your whole visit around a guaranteed sunrise photograph.

Allow **30–60 minutes**, or longer if you enjoy photography.

![Goats and a shepherd beside the river near Shaxi Ancient Town](/assets/blog/dali-hidden-gems-off-the-beaten-path/shaxi-river-goats.webp)

### 4. See the Friday market, if your dates line up

Shaxi's **weekly Friday market (赶集)** is a useful glimpse into everyday life beyond the visitor-oriented old streets. Depending on the season, vendors may sell produce, household goods and locally made products.

The market should not be confused with a permanent tourist market in Sideng Square. Ask your guesthouse to confirm **where the weekly trading is currently happening and when to arrive**. It is usually better to plan for the morning than turn up late in the day.

For market-focused travellers, arriving Thursday and spending Friday morning in town can work well.

### 5. Explore the fields and nearby villages

Do not spend all your time inside the historic square. The surrounding valley has farmland, traditional homes and small village roads that give Shaxi much of its appeal.

A short walk or a locally arranged outing is often enough. Take care around private homes and working farmland, and ask before photographing people closely.

### 6. Add Shibaoshan if you have time

**Shibaoshan (石宝山)** lies outside the town and is known for Buddhist grottoes, temples and mountain scenery. It is not a casual walk from Sideng Square: you need additional transport, and some routes include steps or steep terrain.

A visit is best for travellers interested in history or hiking, rather than something to squeeze into the last hour of a short stop.

## How to get to Shaxi from Dali or Lijiang

Shaxi has **no railway station**. You will need a road transfer even if you use high-speed trains for the larger sections of your Yunnan trip.

### Dali to Shaxi

A direct private-road journey commonly takes around **2–3 hours**, depending on whether you depart from Dali Old Town, the railway station or another part of the lake basin, as well as traffic and road conditions.

- **Private car:** Hotel pickup, luggage space and flexible departure time. The easiest choice for a family, a small group or a continuous Dali–Shaxi–Lijiang journey.
- **Shared transfer:** Can be a useful compromise, but confirm the pickup point, timetable, luggage allowance and exact drop-off point.
- **Public transport:** Connections through the Jianchuan area may be available. Current routes and departure times need checking with a local accommodation provider or operator.

A private car is not essential for every traveller. If you are travelling light and have flexible plans, shared or public transport may suit you.

### Lijiang to Shaxi

Private-road travel from Lijiang often takes around **1.5–2.5 hours**, depending on the pickup point and route. You can also arrange your Yunnan journey in the reverse direction, with Shaxi between Lijiang and Dali.

Public connections are less straightforward to describe as a single permanent timetable. Check the available service for your actual travel date before committing to a tight onward connection.

### Which transport option should you choose?

| Option | Best for | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private vehicle | Families, couples, luggage-heavy trips | Pickup point, stops and whether your driver can reach the guesthouse |
| Shared transfer | Flexible independent travellers | Departure time, luggage policy and final drop-off |

## Where to stay in Shaxi

Your location matters more than how photogenic the property looks in its listing.

### Inside the historic centre

Choose a traditional courtyard or guesthouse close to Sideng Square if you want to walk out for dinner and return easily after dark. Older buildings can have steps, smaller rooms or limited vehicle access, so ask about luggage and stairs.

### Near the edge of town

Staying just outside the old core can make vehicle pickup and luggage handling easier while keeping the square within walking distance. Check the actual walking route, not just the straight-line map distance.

### In the countryside

A guesthouse in the surrounding valley can offer more open views and a quieter setting. You may need additional transport to reach shops and restaurants.

Before booking, ask about heating for colder months, breakfast, room access, vehicle pickup and registration with a foreign passport.

### Half a day: possible, but limited

A short stop allows you to explore the square and nearby lanes. It is not enough to see much beyond the centre, and a same-day return trip from Dali means spending much of your day on the road.

### One night: a good first visit

Arrive from Dali during the afternoon, settle into a courtyard guesthouse, explore the square and walk to Yujin Bridge. The next morning, enjoy a slower start before continuing towards Lijiang.

## When is the best time to visit Shaxi?

**Spring (March–May)** is good for town walks and countryside visits, with comfortable daytime conditions in many periods.

**Summer (June–August)** brings greener landscapes but also rain. Allow flexibility for outdoor excursions and uneven stone paths.

**Autumn (September–November)** can be especially pleasant for valley scenery and exploring on foot. Harvest colours and morning mist vary from year to year.

**Winter (December–February)** is often quieter, but mornings and evenings can be cold. Check that your guesthouse has adequate heating.

Whichever month you choose, avoid scheduling a major transfer immediately after a full day of sightseeing. Shaxi rewards a little breathing room.

## Practical tips for international visitors

- **Save Chinese place names.** 沙溪古镇 is Shaxi Ancient Town; 寺登街 is Sideng Street; 玉津桥 is Yujin Bridge; 兴教寺 is Xingjiao Temple; 石宝山 is Shibaoshan.
- **Set up payments and mobile data.** Make sure your map app, translation tool and mobile payments work before leaving a larger town. See our [China payment guide](/payments-in-china) and [internet guide](/internet-in-china).
- **Bring shoes for stone lanes.** Surfaces can be uneven and slippery when wet.
- **Confirm the final drop-off point.** Not every guesthouse can be reached by car.
- **Check market and attraction arrangements.** Friday market locations, temple hours and Shibaoshan access should be confirmed close to the visit.
- **Respect the town as a place where people live.** Ask before photographing residents closely and do not enter private courtyards without permission.

## Final thoughts: make room for Shaxi rather than squeezing it in

Shaxi does not need a long list of blockbuster attractions to justify a visit. Its value is the relationship between the old market streets, river, villages and the time you give yourself to enjoy them.

If you are travelling between Dali and Lijiang, one overnight stop is a satisfying introduction. For a slower journey, two nights may suit you better. And if your priority lies elsewhere in Yunnan, it is fine to leave Shaxi off the route.
