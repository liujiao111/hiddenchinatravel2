Between Dali Ancient Town and Lijiang Old Town, the road trip is roughly 150–180 km depending on the route; other starting points around Erhai Lake change the distance. Most direct journeys take a few hours, depending on the stations or hotels involved. The train is usually the cheapest option, and a private car is usually the easiest one, especially with kids, more than a couple of suitcases, or a stop somewhere along the way.

One thing worth sorting out before anything else: "Dali" can mean Dali Ancient Town, Xiaguan (downtown Dali), Xizhou, or Shuanglang on Erhai Lake, and all of those sit a different distance from Lijiang. "Lijiang" can mean the railway station, the Old Town, or Shuhe Ancient Town. That's why you'll see slightly different numbers across different guides, including this one. Treat the times below as planning ranges, and check actual train departures through our [12306 booking guide](/book-china-high-speed-rail-foreigners). Confirm bus stops and fares separately in the operator’s ticketing app before you commit to other bookings.

| Option | Typical travel time | Best for | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Train | ~1.5–2.5 hours onboard | Solo travelers, budget trips | Still need a taxi at both ends |
| Bus or station-to-station shuttle | ~3–4 hours (sometimes longer) | Travelers near a convenient stop | Routes, stops and fares vary |

Direct intercity trains commonly take about 1.5–2.5 hours, with second-class fares often around ¥56–80; some tourist trains take longer or use other ticket types. Buses and station-to-station shuttles vary more: allow roughly 3–4 hours or longer, and check the fare rather than assuming a fixed price. These are reference ranges, not a timetable. Check China Railway 12306 for your date and the bus operator’s booking app for current stops and departures.

Short version:

- Take the **train** if getting to Lijiang quickly and cheaply is the whole goal.
- Check a **bus or station shuttle** if a boarding point lines up with your hotels.
- Book a **direct private car** if you want hotel-to-hotel with your luggage handled.
- Book a **private car via Shaxi** if you want time to explore Shaxi with luggage kept in the vehicle. A two-leg station-bus trip may be possible if schedules line up.

## Option 1: By Train

![Dali Railway Station, where trains depart toward Lijiang](/assets/blog/book-china-high-speed-rail-foreigners/dali-railway-station-arrival.webp)

If you just want to get from one city to the other, the train is the easy default. It's quick, reasonably priced, and free from road traffic, though rail disruptions are still possible.

The direct intercity services usually take about 1.5–2.5 hours, with second-class seats often around ¥56–80. Foreign visitors can use an eligible passport to book, but may need to complete passport identity verification in the 12306 system before or during their first booking. Bring the same original document used for the ticket. There are typically several direct departures a day, but the schedule, fare and train type must be checked for your travel date.

A lot of guides skip past one detail: Dali Railway Station sits outside Dali Old Town, and Lijiang Railway Station sits outside Lijiang Old Town too. So "two hours by train" and "two hours door to door" are two different claims. A more realistic breakdown looks like:

- Dali Old Town hotel → taxi to Dali Railway Station: ~30–45 minutes
- Station arrival, security, boarding: ~30–45 minutes
- Train: 1.5–2.5 hours
- Lijiang Railway Station → taxi to Lijiang Old Town or Shuhe: another transfer

Add those transfers and a "two-hour train" can easily take around 3–5 hours door to door; it depends on the actual departure, hotel locations and station waiting time.

This is not a 250–350 km/h high-speed line. The Dali–Lijiang section of the Dali–Lijiang Railway (part of the planned Yunnan–Tibet rail corridor) is a single-track railway with intercity services. Its advertised journey times reflect the line and the train type; do not assume the same speeds as China’s major high-speed routes.

The train suits solo travelers, couples with light luggage, and anyone prioritizing cost over convenience who isn't planning to stop anywhere en route. It's a weaker fit with several suitcases, young kids, or older travelers who'd rather not manage two taxi transfers on top of the ride itself.

## Option 2: By Bus or Station-to-Station Shuttle

Besides conventional long-distance coaches, Dali and Lijiang have had **station-to-station shuttle buses** serving selected stops around Dali Ancient Town, Xizhou, Shuanglang, Lijiang Old Town and Shuhe. The [Dali transport operator announced the service in 2024](https://www.dljt.com.cn/page110?article_id=2415); routes and pickup points can be revised. One published route took around 3 hours 50 minutes, so plan approximately 3–4 hours or longer depending on the service and where you board. Conventional coach tickets are often in the approximate ¥60–110 range, while shuttle fares vary. The ¥29.5 promotional shuttle fares reported in 2024 were **temporary launch offers**, not a current price.

Check the **Station Bus / 站点巴士** listing in DiDi (滴滴出行) and confirm it still serves your intended stops on your date. Do not rely on a 2024 departure chart or assume every bus stops at the same points. If a shuttle stop is close to your hotel, the bus may be more convenient than an old-style coach terminal.

The bus suits budget-conscious travelers with manageable luggage and a convenient pickup point who do not need sightseeing stops on a fixed schedule.

## Option 3: By Private Car

A private car costs more than the train or bus. What it buys you is control over the route and the pace, plus one vehicle for the whole group instead of splitting onto trains and taxis.

### Direct Private Transfer (~2.5–3.5 hours)

This is the straightforward version. Your driver picks you up at an accessible point near your Dali hotel and drops you directly at your Lijiang or Shuhe accommodation, luggage in the vehicle the whole way, mostly via the expressway. It suits:

- Families or groups traveling with multiple suitcases
- Older travelers who'd rather not manage stations and transfers
- Groups splitting the cost of one vehicle
- Anyone arriving or departing at an inconvenient hour for train or bus schedules

Factor in station transfers and taxi waits, and the train's speed edge shrinks fast. A direct private car often lands close to the same door-to-door time.

**On price:** There is no reliable fixed minimum for a private one-way transfer. Your quote depends on the vehicle, travel date, pickup/drop-off point, luggage and whether you want sightseeing time. Ask for a **total agreed price** that specifies fuel, tolls, parking, waiting time, overtime and whether shopping or souvenir stops are included. A low price alone does not prove that a shopping stop is planned; the written terms matter more.

### Scenic Route via Shaxi (usually most of a day)

![Shaxi scenery, Yunnan: a stop on the Dali to Lijiang route](/assets/destinations/yunnan/shaxi/38F5AAF2-AD1C-4D3D-B035-5B9109C589D9.png)

For a lot of travelers, this is the actual reason to book a private car instead of the train. Shaxi Ancient Town is not a quick highway rest stop; a route that includes it looks more like:

```
Dali hotel
→ Shaxi Ancient Town (walk Sifang Street, lunch)
→ Lijiang or Shuhe hotel
```

Allow roughly **6–8 hours in total** for a private transfer via Shaxi with time to walk through the old town and have lunch, and longer if your pickup point is distant or you stay for several hours. The direct hotel-to-hotel drive is often about 2.5–3.5 hours. Treat Shaxi as a full transfer-day plan, not a five-minute roadside stop.

One thing worth flagging: don't try to cram Xizhou, Shuanglang, *and* Shaxi into the same transfer day. Pick one stop and give it enough time to be worth the detour, rather than rushing through all three.

### Another Option: Public Transport via Shaxi

A private car is not the only way to reach Shaxi. **Dali Ancient Town–Shaxi** station-to-station buses were [introduced in January 2026](https://dali.yunnan.cn/system/2026/01/14/033827162.shtml), with the operator quoting about 2.5–3 hours for that leg. **Shaxi–Lijiang** shuttles were [introduced in June 2024](https://www.lijiang.cn/article/131677.html). However, these are separate journeys; availability, exact stops, ticket prices and connection times for a specific date need checking. If you are carrying several suitcases or want a guaranteed smooth transfer, an overnight stay in Shaxi or a prearranged private car is usually simpler. ### Pickup from Xizhou or Shuanglang (Erhai Lake)

If you are staying in Xizhou or Shuanglang on the northern side of Erhai Lake rather than in Dali Old Town, traveling all the way south to Dali Railway Station can create an unnecessary detour. A direct pickup from your Xizhou or Shuanglang hotel skips that detour entirely, and it's one of the clearest cases where booking a private car actually saves time, not just hassle.

## My Practical Recommendation

For a solo traveler or couple with light luggage, the train is usually the sensible default: affordable, quick and straightforward once you account for the station transfers. For a family with children, several large bags or a proper visit to Shaxi, a private car may be worth comparing. The train isn't a bad choice here. The hidden cost of the "cheap" option is two separate taxi rides and a tighter schedule, and that cost gets steeper the more people and bags are involved. The private car earns its price on days when the journey itself is part of the trip, not simply transport between two hotels.
